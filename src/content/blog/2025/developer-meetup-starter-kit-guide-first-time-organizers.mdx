Back in 2018 and 2019, every city in America was flooded with meetups, not just in tech, but for every niche and hobby you could imagine.

In the years since, many of these once-established groups have, after over half a decade now, faded away. But the demand for community has not.

Starting a _new_ meetup is a tough but achievable feat. Here is a full guide to everything you will need to launch your own local developer meetup.

## Table of Contents

## Event and Community Management

As a meetup organizer and community manager, you will need tools to manage and publish your events, and provide a space for your community to interact and grow between events.

### Event Management

Everyone is likely familiar with [Meetup](https://www.meetup.com/). It is still one of the most popular event management tools out there, but they recently dramatically increased their fees for event organizers.

[Lu.ma](https://lu.ma/) has recently gained a lot of popularity, especially in the developer-meetup community. Not only is it free (for free events), but it has great analytics and feedback tools, and allows you to easily email your event guests, also for free.

I would _highly_ recommend looking around the common event management platforms like [Meetup](https://www.meetup.com/), [Lu.ma](https://lu.ma/), and [Eventbrite](https://www.eventbrite.com/), to see what is currently popular in your community.
I found in my city, Meetup was very popular for some types of events, many seemed more like marketing seminars or classes but Lu.ma was much more popular for community-driven events.

### Community Management

Be prepared to foster and grow your community. It's a lot of work to plan and execute meetups, you'll need help. A platform like [Discord](https://discord.com/) or [Slack](https://slack.com/) will keep your community engaged and connect you with the helpers.
People are going to want to help build your community, you'll need a way for everyone to communicate and collaborate.

What platform you pick is ultimately up to you and the type of audience you are trying to reach. Don't pick something too bespoke or difficult to use. People are mostly unwilling to install yet another chat app on their phone. So picking something they already use is a good idea.
Though Discord is not my favorite platform for a number of reasons, it is likely going to be the best choice for most communities, simply because it is so easy for people to join, assuming they already have a Discord account.

### What To Look For

There are things you _need_ in a venue, and things you just _want_. Start by making a list of what's critical for your group, and keep it in mind while scouting. Here are some of the main factors:

#### Cost

Your budget (from $0 and up) is the most important factor to consider at every step of starting your meetup. It's entirely possible to run a small and completely free meetup.
You don't even need anything fancy. Call up a local cafe and ask them what the maximum party size is, and you can simply meet up at some tables and talk.

For a longer-term, it's nice to have a consistent venue and schedule. Many venues (especially cafes) would likely be willing to stay open past their normal hours for a small fee, or possibly even free if there will be enough potential customers for them to stay open.
Don't be scared to ask around for a free venue space. Many local businesses are happy for the exposure and the potential customers, and are often looking out for the local community. If your event is free, make sure to mention that, and see if you can work out a deal.

#### Seating Capacity

Start with your target audience size and make sure the venue can comfortably handle that number, plus a little room to grow. For early meetups, aiming for 25-50 people is a solid baseline.

#### Restrooms

Always check that the venue has restrooms available and that they're easy to access for all attendees.

#### Wi-Fi

An obvious but critical one: make sure reliable internet is available, especially if your events involve live coding, demos, or remote participation.

#### AV Equipment

Think about how presentations will run. Does the space have a projector, large TV, or sound system? Will you need to bring or rent a microphone? Some venues may also require an in-house technician to operate equipment.

#### Food & Drink

Cafés and restaurants may provide food service during or after hours, while other venues may allow you to bring in outside catering. Always confirm policies before you plan around snacks or drinks.

#### Accessibility

Make sure the venue is accessible to everyone. Check for ramps, elevators, or any accommodations that attendees may need.

### How To Find A Venue

There are a few ways to find a venue.

- **Meetup Groups** - Many meetup groups have a preferred venue they use for their events.
- **Local Business Owners** - Many local business owners are happy to host meetups for free in exchange for some exposure.
